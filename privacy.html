1. Who we are

Slayde is operated by Gadgett Beats Jan Rediger, a sole proprietorship registered in Poland. In this policy, "we", "us" and "Slayde" refer to that business. "You" refers to the person using the Slayde panel, whom we also call the producer.

For all questions about your data, including access, correction and deletion requests, write to gadgetbeats99@gmail.com. We answer data requests within thirty days.

2. What Slayde does

Slayde is a web panel for music producers who receive direct messages on Instagram. A producer connects their own Instagram professional account, describes how they want their replies to sound, and Slayde replies on their behalf to people who have messaged them. Replies are generated by an AI assistant that the producer configures. The producer can read every conversation in the panel, switch the assistant off for any conversation, and write replies manually at any time.

Slayde replies inside conversations that another person has started. Instagram's messaging rules allow a business account to reply to a person within twenty four hours of that person's most recent message, and Slayde works inside those rules.

Slayde also includes a research tool that lets a producer review information about artists associated with a Spotify playlist link they submit. That tool is described in section 7.

3. Two different roles, kept separate

This policy covers two different kinds of information, and our legal role is different for each. We do not blur them.

Your account data. We are the controller. Your identity, your billing records, your settings and your Instagram connection are held by us, for our own purposes: running the service, billing you and keeping the service secure. Sections 4, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12 apply, and you may exercise the rights in section 13 directly against us.

The content of your conversations with artists. You are the controller and we process on your behalf. The messages people send you, the replies your assistant sends, and what we derive from them belong to your relationship with those people. We hold that content because you asked us to, we use it only to run the service for you, and we do not use it for our own purposes. Section 5 describes exactly what we hold. A person who messaged you and wants their data removed should ask you first, because you decide; section 14 explains the route and confirms that we will also honour a request sent directly to us.

4. What we hold about you, grouped by why we hold it

4.1 To create and identify your account

You sign in with Google. From that sign-in we store your email address and your name in our own database, together with an internal account identifier and the date your account was created.

Sign-in itself is handled by our authentication provider, Supabase. Its identity record for you comes from Google and may contain further profile fields, such as a profile picture link, that our own application does not read or display.

4.2 Instagram permissions and what we store from them

Our Instagram application is configured with four permissions:

The sign-in screen you see when you connect your account requests two of these, instagram_business_basic and instagram_business_manage_messages. The other two are configured on the application itself.

What our application actually reads from your Instagram profile is three fields: the account identifier, the professional account identifier and the username. We do not request or store your follower list, your photos, your posts, or your contact details.

From connecting your account we store:

  • a long lived access token issued by Meta, held encrypted, together with its expiry date,
  • your Instagram professional account identifier, which is how Instagram addresses incoming messages to your account,
  • an application scoped identifier for your account,
  • your Instagram username, stored in lower case.

4.3 To take payment

Payment is handled by Stripe on pages hosted by Stripe. Your card details are never sent to us, never pass through our software and are never stored by us. We hold only identifiers and status values returned by Stripe: a customer identifier, a subscription identifier, a price identifier, the subscription status, the start and end of the current billing period, the trial end date, the cancellation date if any, and your plan identifiers and usage allowance. We also send Stripe your email address so it can be prefilled on the payment page, and your internal account identifier so that Stripe can tell us which account a payment belongs to.

4.4 To run the assistant the way you configured it

We store the settings you enter in the panel: the persona you write for your assistant, the prompt text your assistant uses in draft and live form, the version history of that prompt text, your quiet hours, your time zone, your reply delay settings, your follower threshold setting, and the master switch that turns the assistant on or off. We also store the Instagram usernames you add to your blocked accounts list.

4.5 To provide the research tool and the learning material

We store the Spotify playlist links you submit and the state of each lookup, your table layout and filter preferences, and which lessons you have marked as completed.

4.6 To keep a record of account deletions

When an account is deleted we write one audit record containing the email address of the deleted account, the IP address the request came from and the browser user agent string. This record does not contain your other data and is kept so that we can show a deletion actually happened. Please read section 12, which is honest about how long it stays.

4.7 Integrations that are not in operation

Our database contains columns intended for future connections to Calendly, Gmail and Google Calendar. Those integrations are not built and not running, and those columns hold no data. We mention this only so that the description of our database is complete. We make no claim about how those columns would be protected, because nothing writes to them today.

5. What we process about people who message you

This is the section a person who messaged a producer should read. Please read section 3 first: for this content, the producer is the controller and we act on their instructions.

5.1 What is stored when a message arrives

When someone sends a direct message to a connected Instagram account, we store:

  • their Instagram account identifier as supplied by Instagram, and, where it is available to us, their Instagram profile name,
  • the text of the message, in readable form,
  • a link to an attachment, if the message carried one. We store the link Instagram gives us, not a copy of the file. When the producer opens that conversation, the attachment is loaded into their browser directly from Meta's servers,
  • the message identifier issued by Instagram, the time the message was sent, and the message type, for example text, image or audio,
  • on the conversation record: whether the assistant is enabled for it, whether the producer has taken it over manually, the time of the most recent activity in it, and the classification fields described in section 5.4.

We do not store their profile picture, their follower count, their email address, their phone number or any other part of their Instagram profile.

Messages are stored whether or not the assistant replies. The producer's settings decide whether a reply is generated; they do not decide whether the message is recorded, because the producer needs to be able to read their own inbox.

5.2 What is stored when a reply is sent

For every reply, whether generated by the assistant or written by the producer, we store the text that was sent, the recipient's Instagram identifier, the message identifier returned by Instagram, and whether delivery succeeded or failed together with any error message Instagram returned.

5.3 Message content is sent to OpenAI to generate a reply

When the assistant is due to reply, we send the following to OpenAI:

  • up to thirty of the most recent messages from that conversation, including the message being answered, in readable form,
  • the prompt text the producer configured for their assistant.

OpenAI returns the reply text and a short structured assessment of the conversation. The model used is GPT-4o. We do not send OpenAI your Instagram token, your billing data or anything from other conversations.

5.4 What we derive and store about a conversation

From the assistant's assessment we store five judgement fields per conversation, plus the time they were last updated:

FieldWhat it records
Lead temperatureA short label describing how promising the conversation looks, for example cold, warm, hot, converted or disqualified
Current sentimentHow the person appears to be responding right now
Engagement trendWhether their engagement is growing or fading across the conversation
Funnel stepA number indicating how far the conversation has progressed
Meeting confirmedWhether a meeting appears to have been agreed

Alongside these, the conversation record itself carries a status label that the producer can set by hand, from the same range of values, and a timestamp of the most recent activity, which the database updates whenever any message is added to the conversation, in either direction. We also keep a log of the status changes a producer makes by hand, and the panel counts how many messages a conversation contains.

The assistant produces further information while generating a reply, and none of that further information is written to our database.

5.5 Message content and readability

Message content is stored in our database in readable form. It is not encrypted at the level of the individual field. It is protected by the access controls described in section 10.

5.6 Notifications about comments

Because our application is configured for comment access, Instagram may send us notifications about comments left on a producer's posts, including comments on live videos. Such a notification identifies the Instagram account it concerns and carries whatever comment details Instagram chooses to include.

We do not store anything from these notifications. When one arrives, our software checks the shape of the notification and looks up which account it belongs to, finds no direct message inside it, and stops. No comment text, no commenter identity and no comment identifier is written to our database. What remains in our own systems is a server log line noting that a notification arrived for that account, which names the producer's account and nothing about the person who left the comment. Like any web request, the request itself may also be recorded by the infrastructure that hosts our servers, under that provider's own logging rules.

6. Automated decision making

The assistant generates reply text and the assessment fields in section 5.4 automatically. These are not decisions that produce legal effects or similarly significant effects on anyone. The producer can read every conversation, override or switch off the assistant, and reply manually. No automated decision is made about a person's access to credit, employment, or any comparable matter.

7. Artist information in the research tool

This is a research and discovery tool for reviewing artist information. It does not send messages.

If a producer submits a Spotify playlist link, information about the artists associated with that playlist is collected on the producer's behalf and stored so they can review it in a table. The fields we hold are: artist name, Spotify profile link, top track names and identifiers, monthly listener counts, Instagram handle, genre labels and biography text.

As at the date of this policy we hold no email addresses in this tool.

An artist who wants their entry removed can write to gadgetbeats99@gmail.com and we will remove it.

10. Security

Your Instagram access token is encrypted. It is encrypted inside the database using symmetric encryption, with a key that is generated inside the database and stored in a managed secret store. The key never leaves the database. Only our server side processes can obtain the decrypted token; it is never sent to the browser and is not readable through the panel. Writing a token is restricted to your own account row and cannot be pointed at anyone else's.

Every table that holds account data is protected by row level access rules, enforced by the database itself. These rules govern everything the panel does on your behalf while you are signed in, so they cover the ordinary path by which data is read and written.

Some server side operations run with a privileged database credential that these rules do not restrict. They have to: when a message arrives from Instagram there is no signed in user, and the first thing the server must do is look up which account the message belongs to, which is a question that cannot be answered from within a single account. Once the account is identified, the operations that read and write that account's data are written to name that account explicitly in every query. The safeguard for these paths is our own code and our own review of it, not the database.

Billing fields cannot be written from the browser. Your subscription status, plan allowance and payment identifiers can only be written by our server after Stripe has confirmed the change with a signed message. A logged in user cannot alter them.

Incoming requests from Meta are verified cryptographically before anything is read from them or written to the database.

We do not claim our systems are impossible to breach. If a breach affects your data we will notify you and the relevant supervisory authority as required by law.

12. How long we keep data

We are going to be plain about this, because a vague answer here would be misleading.

No automatic deletion runs anywhere in our systems today. There is no scheduled job, no expiry timer and no retention process that removes data after a period of time. We have verified this: no scheduled database task exists, no scheduled server function is deployed, and no background workflow is enabled.

This means:

  • Your account data stays until you delete your account. When you delete it, the deletion happens immediately and is not reversible.
  • Conversation content, including message text and the assessment fields, stays until you delete the conversation or delete your account. Deleting a conversation removes its messages, its sent message records and its assessments at the same time.
  • The Instagram connection is cleared as soon as you disconnect it in the panel, or when you remove Slayde's access from inside Instagram, or when Meta sends us a data deletion request for that account.
  • The account deletion audit record described in section 4.6 stays after your account is gone. It holds an email address, an IP address and a user agent string. We intend to keep such records for one year, but we are telling you honestly that no process currently deletes them automatically. If you want yours removed, write to us and we will remove it.

If you would like anything else removed sooner, section 14 explains how to ask.

  • access the personal data we hold about you and receive a copy,
  • correct data that is inaccurate or incomplete,
  • delete your data, subject to the limits we describe honestly in section 14,
  • restrict or object to our processing,
  • portability, meaning a copy of data you provided to us in a machine readable form,
  • withdraw consent where our processing rests on consent,
  • complain to a supervisory authority. In Poland this is the President of the Personal Data Protection Office, Prezes Urzędu Ochrony Danych Osobowych, https://uodo.gov.pl.

Our legal bases are: performance of our contract with you, for running your account, your subscription and your Instagram connection; our legitimate interests, for keeping the service secure and for the deletion audit record; and legal obligation, for tax and accounting records held by our payment provider.

For conversation content, the producer is the controller. If you messaged a producer and want to exercise a right over that content, please read section 14.

We have not appointed a data protection officer, and we are not required to appoint one.

14. How to exercise your rights, including deletion

Write to gadgetbeats99@gmail.com. We respond within thirty days.

Detailed step by step instructions for every deletion route are on our data deletion instructions page, which is the page linked from our Instagram app listing. In summary:

  • If you are a producer with a Slayde account, you can delete your entire account yourself in the panel, under Account Settings. You can also delete any single conversation from the conversation list.
  • If you removed Slayde from your Instagram account, or Meta sent us a deletion request for your Instagram account, we clear your stored Instagram connection: the access token, both account identifiers and the stored username. Please note the limit clearly: this clears the connection, it does not by itself delete conversations already stored in the producer's inbox. To remove those, delete the conversations or the account in the panel, or write to us.
  • If you are someone who messaged a producer, the producer controls that conversation and can delete it from their panel. Ask them first. You can also write to us at the address above and we will act on your request and pass it to the producer concerned.
